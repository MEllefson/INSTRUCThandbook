Chapter 2 General Information

INSTRUCT Lab: Implementing New Student Thinking Resources Using Cognitive Theory

2.1 Welcome to INSTRUCT

All students who do research with Prof Michelle Ellefson and/or Dr Hale Ögel-Balaban become members of INSTRUCT. We are excited to work with you on your journey of exploring research in Psychology of Education (cognitive science).

Over the years, we have found that we tend to offer similar sorts of advice to students, this handbook is a result of various drafts and feedback from current/previous students. It is also a place where expectations for you are outlined and structured. These expectations have developed to provide you opportunities to get as much out of your limited supervision time as possible. Here’s to a terrific year!

However, before getting too much into the details of how we work together, take a moment to reflect on our core values. In INSTRUCT we: LEARN from each other; SUPPORT each other, and SHARE with each other. That means that INSTRUCT is a group where each of us are both seeking advice as well as giving it. Each one of us becomes a better researcher if we are jointly working towards building up our research community. There are going to be many times during your time with us where you will benefit from the work of others. In return, we ask that you pay it forward and leave a legacy for those who arrive after you.

2.2 Diversity and Inclusion

Our world, our university, and our research field are a long way from being perfect examples of diversity and inclusion. There is a real need to mentor early career researchers from all backgrounds. Hale/Michelle are committed to improving that diversity and sees it a strength that INSTRUCT includes researchers from diverse backgrounds that represent a variety of socioeconomic, national, linguistic, ethnic, religious, gender identities, sexualities and more.

Running a diverse team can mean that we are not always fully aware of each other’s experiences. That lack of knowledge can sometimes be challenging for all of us. However, understanding each other is a key part of avoiding microaggressions and creating a team that is welcoming for us all. If you are concerned in any way that there is systemic bullying or discrimination occurring within INSTRUCT, then please bring this incident to our attention in any way that makes you most comfortable.

2.3 Effective Collaborations Use Shared Working Practices

A common approach to INSTRUCT work makes it easier for new members to integrate into the lab. In addition, shared working practices means that Hale/Michelle can focus on the academic/research aspects of being your supervisor/PI/mentor and they do not need to spend your limited supervision time trying to figure out where you have put your files or other things that distract from your work. Making Hale/Michelle’s work as efficient as possible has a direct benefit to you as then they can spend your limited supervision time focused on your work/research.

However, just because we are striving for consistency across the group, that does not mean that any given approach outlined here is the most efficient. If you feel that there is something that we as a team can do more effectively, then please bring that up during INSTRUCT meetings. We can discuss that strategy and agree whether to adopt it together. Many of the details in this handbook are the result of such discussions, so your views for change are welcomed, and potential changes will be considered in terms of their utility for the full group.

2.4 Mistakes are: Expected, Respected, Inspected, Corrected

INSTRUCT members want to conduct research that is: * Rigorous * Ethical * Transparent, easy to replicate * Contribute to knowledge/literature, novel, fill gaps * Worth reading, informative, thought provoking.

We work at the cutting edge of knowledge in a detail-oriented field where learning is by doing. This means that mistakes are common. All INSTRUCT work is carefully checked for mistakes, because we all make them (including Prof Ellefson and Dr Ögel-Balaban!). We pay great attention to detail in all aspects of our work and dissemination because the quality of our work impacts our reputation. Making mistakes is part of learning to do science. The key is to correct any mistakes we find and to keep looking out for them in all stages of the research process.
